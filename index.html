What is Top Down Space?

Top Down Space is a fast, top-down free-for-all. You pilot a lead ship around an enclosed arena, grow it into a fleet by collecting power-ups, and fight other fleets — human or AI — until only one commander is left flying.

Every match runs the same three phases:

  1. Setup — choose how many human players (1–3), how many bot fleets (0–100), a bot difficulty tier, and any modules to enable.
  2. Countdown & Battle — a 3-second countdown, then free-for-all combat until one fleet remains.
  3. Victory — the last surviving commander is declared the winner. Press any key, or use the buttons, to rematch or return to the menu.

Objective

Be the last fleet standing. A fleet is eliminated when it has zero ships left (or, under the 3 Lives module, after its lives run out). There are no teams — everyone, human and bot alike, fights everyone else.

The Arena

Each match spawns fresh terrain: a starfield and drifting nebula for backdrop, 3 indestructible obstacles that block movement and bounce bullets, and up to 15–30 asteroids that drift, spin, and split when destroyed. Hazard and rule modules (see the Modules tab) can add gravity wells, more/bigger asteroids, quartered arenas, and more.

New here? Start a 1 Player match against a couple of Standard bots with no modules to get a feel for movement and formations before diving into modifiers.

Flight Model

Ships fly tank-style: the move keys accelerate forward or backward along your current facing, and the turn keys rotate that facing. Your lead ship coasts to a stop when you let go of the throttle — unless Zero-G Drift is active, in which case you keep momentum until you actively brake.

Firing

You never press a fire button — every ship in your fleet fires automatically on a cooldown, in the direction it's facing. A bigger fleet fires very slightly slower per-ship (cooldown scales up ~1.5% per extra ship) to keep huge fleets from becoming pure bullet hoses.

Dash

Tap your dash key for a burst of +4.5 speed lasting a quarter-second, on an 1.8-second cooldown. Dashing is your main dodge and gap-closer — it also leaves a bright particle trail behind your lead ship, both to look good and to telegraph to everyone on screen (bots included) that you just committed to a movement.

Changing Formation

Tap your formation key to cycle your escort ships to the next formation shape in the sequence. See the Fleet & Formations tab for what each one does.

Lead Ship vs. Escorts

Your first ship is the lead ship — the only one you steer directly. Every other ship in your fleet is an escort that automatically holds a position relative to the lead ship, based on your active formation, and always fires in whatever direction its formation slot points.

Each ship carries its own HP pool independently (1000 HP by default, 300 HP for ships gained mid-match from power-ups). Losing your lead ship doesn't end your run — the next ship in line effectively becomes your new steering reference the moment it's the only one left, and you fight on until your entire fleet is destroyed.

Growing Your Fleet

Power-ups spawn periodically around the arena worth +2, +4, +6, +8, or +10 ships (smaller values are more common). Fly any ship in your fleet into one to instantly add that many escorts at your current position, up to the fleet cap (100 ships, or 1000 under the Max Ships 1000 module).

Formations

Formations only change where your escorts sit and which way they aim — every escort fires every volley regardless of formation, so no formation deals more raw damage per volley than another. The difference is entirely about shot direction, footprint, and how easy your fleet is to read.

Standard Formations

Roman's Expansion Formations

Enabling the Roman's Expansion module replaces the standard rotation with six more deliberate, shape-based formations, each with its own firing pattern:

Strategy — Roman's Disguise: Disguise collapses your whole fleet onto the exact position of your lead ship and locks every escort's cannon to fire in the lead ship's direction. Total damage output is unchanged (every ship still fires every volley) — what you gain is that your fleet reads as a single small ship to onlookers (including bots), so enemies routinely misjudge your real HP pool and firepower until they're already in your kill zone. The trade-off: with the whole fleet stacked on one point, a single well-placed collision or explosion can catch several escorts in quick succession, so it rewards aggressive, decisive play over a long drawn-out brawl.

Asteroids

Asteroids drift and spin freely, bounce off arena walls and obstacles, and split into two half-sized, lower-HP fragments when destroyed (unless already small). They deal contact damage to any ship that touches them, so weaving through a field is a real risk, not just scenery.

Obstacles

Three static obstacles spawn away from starting positions each match. They physically block ships and ricochet bullets — useful as cover, but also capable of bouncing a shot back the way it came.

Gravity Wells (module)

When active, three wells pull every ship and bullet in the arena toward their centers with inverse-square force — strong up close, negligible far away. They warp both approach lines and bullet trajectories, rewarding pilots who route around a well to sling-shot past it rather than fighting the pull head-on.

Modules

Modules are optional rule modifiers you toggle before launch. They're saved between sessions, so your last loadout is remembered next time you open this menu. The launch screen also offers presets — Vanilla, Chaos, Tactical, Bullet Hell, Zen, and Hardcore — one-click bundles that set a whole loadout at once; picking any individual module afterward simply edits from there. A search bar on that screen filters modules by name or effect. Below is every module in extreme detail — the short card in the launch menu is intentionally the summary; this is the full explanation.

Reading Bot Behavior

Bots aren't random — they run a small state machine that re-evaluates roughly every reaction time (Novice ~450ms down to Deity ~40ms) and settles into one of six states:

  • Patrolling — no threat or opportunity nearby; wanders to random points.
  • Chasing / Aggressive Chase — an enemy is within the bot's style-based chase range. Deity bots above 75% fleet HP upgrade to Aggressive Chase, which extends their effective firing range further.
  • Power-Up Chase — a power-up is within awareness range and the bot's fleet is under half the ship cap. Bots stop actively growing once they're past that halfway point, favoring combat instead.
  • Retreating — total fleet HP has dropped under the bot's own retreat threshold (see below — this is no longer one fixed number). The bot runs toward a random point near arena-center rather than a fixed safe zone.
  • Evading — an enemy bullet is within 60 units of the bot's lead ship, regardless of health. This is a hard reflex, not a health check.
Exploit it: Since Evading triggers purely on bullet proximity, a single well-aimed shot can interrupt a bot's chase entirely — useful for buying a few seconds against a fleet that outguns you. And since Retreating sends bots toward the arena's center rather than a real hiding spot, a wounded bot is often more predictable to finish off than a healthy one.

Know Your Opponent — Personality & Color

Every bot is one of four playing styles, and — this matters — each style has its own signature color family. You can read a bot's temperament from across the arena before it ever fires a shot:

  • Reds / oranges — Aggressive. Largest chase range, biggest speed boost when hunting, and it keeps fighting down to roughly 25% fleet HP before it even considers breaking off. Treat any red ship as committed the moment it notices you.
  • Cyans / blues — Defensive. Shortest chase range and the earliest retreat threshold of the four (about 45%). A blue bot that starts losing an exchange will often bail well before it's actually in danger — press an early advantage and it may simply leave.
  • Golds / yellows — Dashy. Frequent, aggressive dashing and the fastest speed boost in pursuit, with a mid-range retreat threshold (~30%). Predict its dash cooldown rather than its current position — it won't be where it looks like it's headed.
  • Violets / purples — Formation. Favors coordinated positioning over raw speed or aggression, retreats around 35% HP. Most likely to actually make good use of Roman's Expansion formations if that module is active.

Each bot also carries its own name (Reaper, Warden, Flash, Tactician, and so on) drawn from its style — the Combat Log on the right will call bots out by name, and their color in the fleet list and on the radar always matches their personality family.

Aim Locks & Angles

Bots only fire when they're aimed close enough to their target — within roughly 17° for Deity, and roughly 40° for everyone else. Strafing perpendicular to a non-Deity bot, rather than fleeing straight backward, denies it a firing angle far more effectively than raw distance does. Deity's tighter window and near-zero aim error make this much harder to pull off against the top tier.

General Tactics

  • Bait power-ups. Bots under half fleet cap will break off combat to chase a power-up — contest one deliberately to pull a bot out of position.
  • Fight near obstacles, not asteroids. Obstacles bounce bullets and block movement without damaging you; asteroids damage everyone who touches them, friend or foe.
  • Use formations defensively in the open, offensively in a corner. A spread formation (Swarm, Diamond) is harder to alpha-strike with one shot; a stacked one (Disguise, Compact) concentrates your presence when you're about to commit to a kill.
  • Watch the radar. The HUD's tactical radar shows every living fleet and hazard at a glance — use it to avoid getting sandwiched between two chasers.
  • Dash to deny, not just to flee. A dash through a bot's firing line breaks its angle lock and often forces it out of an aim-lock state entirely.
  • Target the color you can beat. In a mixed bot lobby, a Defensive (cyan) bot under pressure often disengages before it dies — it's the easiest style to rout, not necessarily the easiest to kill. An Aggressive (red) bot rarely gives you that option.
  • Track HP bars, not just positions. Every lead ship now shows a small health bar overhead — glance at it before committing to a fight instead of guessing from how long an exchange has gone on.

New Module Tactics

  • Burst Fire rewards patience: don't trigger a burst the instant a target is in view — wait for the moment you expect it to still be in your sights a quarter-second later, since you're committed to all three shots and then a real gap in your fire.
  • Homing Shots matters most at medium range — up close it can't turn fast enough to matter, and at long range the target usually moves out of the curve radius first. Use it to punish targets crossing your firing line rather than ones running straight away.
  • Overdrive turns your dash into an offensive tool: dash into a firefight, not just out of one, and use the damage-resistance window to force a trade you'd otherwise lose.
  • Ricochet Rounds makes edges and corners dangerous for everyone, including you — funneling a fight toward the arena's center denies the enemy free wall bounces just as much as it denies you.
  • Debris Field punishes greedy asteroid clearing — if you're farming a dense asteroid cluster for splits, plan an exit line before you start, not after the cloud is already there.
